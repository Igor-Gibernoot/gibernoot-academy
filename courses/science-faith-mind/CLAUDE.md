# Course `science-faith-mind`: Science, Faith and Mind

Igor's course on the philosophy of science, theology and the study of mind and religion: reality,
knowledge, God, consciousness, sacred texts and the major traditions. **There is no main book**:
the spine is `course.yaml` (24 levels) and the references are in `REFERENCES.md`. Everything in
the root `CLAUDE.md` applies; this file adds what is specific to this course.

## Purpose and stance (the heart of the course)

- **The goal is the right questions, not answers.** The course shows several ways to see each
  problem and how to approach it; it never declares a winner on God, religion, consciousness or
  reality. Each lesson ends by naming where thoughtful people part ways and *why* (which premise,
  which value, which kind of evidence). The closing "what you now own" card says what was
  clarified and what stays open.
- **Steelman every position.** Present each view in the form its best defenders would accept,
  then its strongest objection, then the best reply. A believer, an atheist, an agnostic and a
  scientist should each read the lesson and say "that is fair". Never mock, never moralise.
- **Separate three layers in every claim** and label them: (1) *established fact* (data, texts,
  history), (2) *scholarly consensus or live debate* (say which), (3) *faith or metaphysical
  commitment*. Never pass a layer-3 claim off as layer 1, in either direction.
- **Arguments as guided proofs.** Each classical argument (cosmological, kalam, ontological,
  fine-tuning, evil, hard problem, zombie, wager, miracles...) is a guided proof (`proof: true`):
  numbered premises, each with a question "which premise would a critic reject, and why?", the
  validity step checked separately from the truth of the premises.
- **Questions that test understanding, not allegiance.** Cards ask "what does this argument
  assume?", "which reply answers this objection?", "what would count as evidence against it?",
  never "is God real?". Wrong options encode real misunderstandings (feedback explains the slip).
- **Always say "according to".** Every claim is taught *according to* someone or something: this
  sacred book, this thinker, this tradition, this argument, this experiment, this school of
  historians. Name the source in the sentence, so the limit of the answer is visible (not "the soul
  survives death" but "according to the Phaedo...", "according to Spiritism...", "according to
  physicalists..."). Questions use the same frame ("According to Hume, why...").
- **Offer two or more views whenever possible.** Show how the same thing can be seen in several ways:
  at least two readings of a text, two interpretations of a result, two replies to an objection.
  A card that asks for one view should have the rival view in its feedback or the card after.
- **Respect for living traditions.** Describe a tradition as its adherents do ("Christians hold...",
  "the Qur'an states..."), then give the scholarly view as scholarly ("historians conclude..."),
  and keep the two visible. Use the tradition's own terms with a plain gloss.
- **Odd claims get honest standards, not scorn** (altered states, mediumship, parapsychology,
  Spiritism, UAPs): what is claimed, what is documented, what would test it, what tests found.

## Physics and maths depth

Conceptual first, technical only where a number or equation carries the argument (CHSH bound
$2\sqrt2$, Bayes' rule, the Drake equation, fine-tuning ratios, entropy odds). Physics is stated as
physics knows it today, with what is established kept apart from interpretation (quantum
foundations, cosmology of the beginning). Every number is checked by script and its source named.

## Sources and verification

No `sources/` book. `REFERENCES.md` lists trusted works per level. Each lesson's claims are
checked by (1) deriving or arguing them in the lesson, (2) a script for every number, and (3) a
cross-check against the level's references (use WebSearch/WebFetch for dates, quotations,
figures). Quotations and attributions must be accurate: if unsure, paraphrase and say so. Log
what was checked in the `Verified` line of `STATUS.md` (compact). If Igor later adds a book to
`sources/` (git-ignored), follow the root workflow.

## Trail

`course.yaml`: 24 levels in three parts. Part I (1-7): knowing, reality, mathematics, origins and
the arguments about God. Part II (8-13): nature of God, science and religion, mind, altered states,
experience, why humans believe. Part III (14-24): Judaism and the Torah, Christianity and the Gospels, Islam and the Qur'an, Hindu,
Buddhist, other and Spiritist traditions, the cosmic perspective, faith and knowledge, philosophies of
life, meaning, ethics and death.
**Three exams (course-specific, replaces the root two-exam rule):** `exam-partial1` after Level 7
(covers 2-7), `exam-partial2` after Level 13 (covers 8-13), `exam-final` after Level 24 (covers
14-24). All original (no professor's exam). The next unit after Level 7, 13 or 24 is its exam.
Status: `STATUS.md`. Build Levels 1 to 3 first, then review the tone with Igor.

## Notation and conventions

Names of thinkers with dates at first use (once per lesson). Logic: premises numbered P1, P2...,
conclusion C. Probability $P(H\mid E)$ and Bayes' rule as in `CONVENTIONS.md`. Sacred-text
quotations: give book, chapter, verse; translation named; short. Non-English terms in italics
with a gloss (*tawhid*, *anatta*, *moksha*). Use "God" for the concept under discussion and say
whose God when it matters. Dates: BCE/CE.
Intro level glossary words (gloss at first use): evidence, argument, premise, theory, hypothesis,
faith, reason, belief, knowledge, consciousness, mind, soul, God, religion, science, philosophy,
theology, metaphysics, atheist, agnostic, theist.
