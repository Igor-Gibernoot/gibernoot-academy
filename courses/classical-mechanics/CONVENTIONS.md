# classical-mechanics conventions by chapter

Notation, running examples and key numbers fixed while building. Read only the section you need: `grep -n '^## '`, then `sed -n` that range.

## General

- One notation for the whole course; where Taylor and Goldstein differ, Goldstein's symbol ($L$, $H$, $V$, $q_j$, $p_j$), named once against the other book's. To confirm against the sources.
- **Polar angle in the plane: $\varphi$** (Taylor's symbol; $\theta$ is already the slope angle of Level 2 and the spherical polar angle later), $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}$ as unit vectors. Used in Levels 2-13. At Level 14+ say once that Goldstein writes $\theta$ for it. Exception to the Goldstein-first rule, chosen because Taylor is the main source for Levels 1-13.
- SI units; $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ unless a lesson says otherwise.

## Chapter 1
- Pendulum phase portrait (widget `phase-portrait`): $E = p^2/2 + 1 - \cos q$, $q$ = angle from hanging down, bottom $E = 0$, separatrix $E = 2$, units $m = l = g = 1$.

- Moon: $r = 3.84\times10^8$ m, $T = 2.36\times10^6$ s, $a = 4\pi^2 r/T^2 = 2.72\times10^{-3}$ m/s$^2$ = $g(R/r)^2$ at $r = 60R$; Earth $R = 6371$ km, $v_c \approx 7.9$ km/s.
- $\mathbf{v} = \dot{\mathbf{r}}$, $\mathbf{a} = \ddot{\mathbf{r}}$, $\mathbf{F} = m\ddot{\mathbf{r}}$; the state = position and velocity at one instant; time step $\Delta t$.
- Action $S = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} L\,dt$, $L = T - V$ (Goldstein writes $I$ in 2.1; the course uses $S$); kinetic $T = \tfrac12 m\dot y^2$, potential $V = mgy$, $g = 10$ m/s$^2$ in the intro toy numbers. Intro examples: thrown ball $m = 1$ kg ground to ground in 2 s (peak 5 m, $S = -33.3$ J s); free ball 2 m in 2 s ($S = 1$ vs 2); lifeguard 5 and 1.5 m/s (entry 52 m vs 30 m). Hamilton's principle is stationary, not least (spring $k = 1$, 4 s).
- Challenge: lifeguard A (0,12) m, B (20,-8) m, 5 and 1.5 m/s, best crossing 17.94 m (9.82 s); kicked pendulum p = 1.6, E = 1.28.
- Phase space intro: $q$ position, $p$ momentum (here $p = \dot q$, $m = 1$); periods of the pendulum: 6.35 ($q_0 = 0.4$), 7.30 (1.5), 16.16 (3), spinner $p = 3$ gives 2.41; spring $2\pi$; separatrix $E = 2$.
- Challenge part 2: thrown ball m = 1 kg, g = 10, ground to ground in 3 s, family y = a t(3-t), S(a) = 4.5 a^2 - 45 a, real peak 11.25 m (S = -112.5); hill V = -q^2/2, $\ddot q = q$, q = q0 cosh t, from q0 = 0.1 reaches q = 2 at t = 3.69, from 0.001 at 8.29; E = 0 lines are the hill's separatrices.

## Chapter 2
- Frames $S$ (platform, bank) and $S'$ (train, water), constant relative velocity $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}' + \mathbf{u}t$, $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a}'$. Train 20 m/s, ball 3 m/s, wall 6 m; river 4 m/s, boat 3 m/s (speed 5).
- Taylor notation: $\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{y}},\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ or $\mathbf{e}_i$, components $r_i$; torque $\boldsymbol{\Gamma} = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$. Thrown ball $x = 4t$, $y = 10t - 5t^2$ (g = 10 for round numbers).
- Inertia: $\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{F}=\dot{\mathbf{p}}$; frame $S'$ with origin $\mathbf{R}(t)$ in inertial $S$: $\mathbf{a}'=\mathbf{a}-\ddot{\mathbf{R}}$; braking cart $\ddot R=-2$ m/s$^2$; Earth equator rotation $0.034$ m/s$^2$ (0.35% of $g$), orbit $0.006$ m/s$^2$; sliding $d=v_0^2/(2g\mu)$, $v_0=10$ m/s: 7.3 m ($\mu=0.7$), 51 m (0.1), 255 m (0.02).
- Third law: $\mathbf{F}_{\alpha\beta}$ = force on $\alpha$ by $\beta$, $\mathbf{F}_{12} = -\mathbf{F}_{21}$ (different bodies); $\mathbf{P} = \sum\mathbf{p}_\alpha$, $\dot{\mathbf{P}} = \mathbf{F}^{\text{ext}}$; rifle 4 kg, bullet 0.02 kg at 600 m/s, recoil 3 m/s; $F_{\text{mag}}/F_{\text{Coul}} \sim v^2/c^2$.
- Block on an incline (Taylor 1.6): origin at the release point, $x$ down the slope, $y$ normal (away from the slope), $z$ across; angle $\theta$, $\mathbf{w}=m\mathbf{g}$, $N = mg\cos\theta$, $f=\mu_k N$ sliding, static $f\le\mu_s N$ (equals $mg\sin\theta$ at rest), $\ddot x = g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)$, slides iff $\tan\theta>\mu_s$. Numbers: 2 kg, 30 deg, $\mu_k=0.2$: $f=3.39$ N, $\ddot x=3.20$, $x(2)=6.4$ m; $\arctan0.5=26.57^\circ$.
- Polar (Taylor 1.7): $x=r\cos\varphi$, $y=r\sin\varphi$; $\mathbf{r}=r\hat{\mathbf{r}}$; $d\hat{\mathbf{r}}/dt=\dot\varphi\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}$, $d\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}/dt=-\dot\varphi\hat{\mathbf{r}}$; $\mathbf{v}=\dot r\hat{\mathbf{r}}+r\dot\varphi\hat{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}$; $a_r=\ddot r-r\dot\varphi^2$, $a_\varphi=r\ddot\varphi+2\dot r\dot\varphi$ (Coriolis term $2\dot r\dot\varphi$, named, treated in the rotating-frames level); $\omega=\dot\varphi$. Numbers: stone $0.2$ kg, $r=0.5$ m, $\dot\varphi=4$ rad/s: $a_r=-8$, $T=1.6$ N ($6.4$ N at 8 rad/s); $r=2$, $\dot\varphi=1.5$: $a_r=-4.5$.
- Polar proof: $\hat{\mathbf r}=\cos\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf x}+\sin\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf y}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol\varphi}=-\sin\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf x}+\cos\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf y}$; $a_\varphi=\frac1r\frac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot\varphi)$; free line $x=2$ m, $\dot y=3$ m/s: at $y=0$ $\ddot r=4.5$, $\dot\varphi=1.5$, $a_r=0$; circle $r=0.25$ m, $\dot\varphi=6$: $a_r=-9$.
- Challenge part 1: cart R-ddot = 1.5 m/s^2, crate 2 kg, mu_s 0.4 (limit 3.92 m/s^2), mu_k 0.3 at R-ddot = 5 gives a' = -2.06; skaters 40 and 60 kg, J = 120 N s over 0.5 s (240 N), rope 20 N for 2 s gives P = 40; bead 0.1 kg on r = 0.5 + t, phi = 2t, F = (-0.6, 0.4) N at t = 1.
- Challenge part 2: dumbbell 1 kg and 3 kg on a 1.2 m rod (CM 0.9 and 0.3 m from the ends), CM at 2 m/s on y = 2, omega 4: L_spin 4.32, orbital -16, total -11.68 (z out of page); reel in to half radii gives omega 16; puff J = 3 N s on the 1 kg end of the rod at rest: V 0.75, omega 2.5 (clockwise), 3 kg end at rest. Notation s_alpha, u_alpha = position, velocity relative to the CM.
- Challenge part 2: block launched up a 30 deg slope, v0 = 3 m/s, mu_s 0.7, mu_k 0.4: deceleration 8.29, distance 0.5425 m, stays at rest; puck 0.25 kg at r = 0.5 m on a string to a hanging 0.4 kg: T = 3.92 N, phi-dot = 5.6 rad/s; at r-dot = -0.1, phi-ddot = 2.24 rad/s^2.

## Chapter 3
- Drag (Taylor 2.1): $\mathbf{f} = -f(v)\hat{\mathbf{v}}$, $f = bv + cv^2 = f_{\text{lin}} + f_{\text{quad}}$; sphere $b = \beta D$, $c = \gamma D^2$, air STP $\beta = 1.6\times10^{-4}$ N s/m$^2$, $\gamma = 0.25$ N s$^2$/m$^4$; ratio $f_{\text{quad}}/f_{\text{lin}} = (1.6\times10^3\ \mathrm{s/m^2})Dv$. Fall: down positive, $m\dot v = mg - f(v)$; widget toy body $m = 1$ kg, $b = 1$ kg/s, $c = 0.3$ kg/m (not a real sphere). Numbers: baseball 7 cm at 5 m/s: 560 (about 600); raindrop 1 mm, 0.6 m/s: 0.96; oil droplet: $1.2\times10^{-7}$.
- Linear drag (Taylor 2.2): $v_{\text{ter}} = mg/b$, $\tau = m/b$, $v = v_{\text{ter}} + (v_0 - v_{\text{ter}})e^{-t/\tau}$; cart $x_\infty = v_{x0}\tau$; terminal speed written $v_{\text{ter}}$ in lessons (widget label `vt: 'v_ter'`).
- Projectile with linear drag (Taylor 2.3): $y$ up, so $y(t) = (v_{y0}+v_{\text{ter}})\tau(1-e^{-t/\tau}) - v_{\text{ter}}t$; $R_{\text{vac}} = 2v_{x0}v_{y0}/g$; $R \approx R_{\text{vac}}(1 - \tfrac43 v_{y0}/v_{\text{ter}})$, good for $v_{y0}/v_{\text{ter}} \ll 1$. Running toy throw: $m = 1$ kg, $b = 0.1$ kg/s, 20 m/s at 45 deg ($\tau = 10$ s, $v_{\text{ter}} = 98$ m/s, exact $R = 34.10$ m).
- Quadratic drag (Taylor 2.4): $m\dot v = mg - cv^2$, $v_{\text{ter}} = \sqrt{mg/c}$, $v = v_{\text{ter}}\tanh(gt/v_{\text{ter}})$, $y = (v_{\text{ter}}^2/g)\ln\cosh(gt/v_{\text{ter}})$; coasting $v = v_0/(1+t/\tau)$, $\tau = m/(cv_0)$ (not the linear $\tau$), $x = v_0\tau\ln(1+t/\tau)$. Running baseball: $m = 0.15$ kg, $D = 7$ cm, $c = 1.225\times10^{-3}$ kg/m, $v_{\text{ter}} = 34.6$ m/s, $y(6\,\mathrm{s}) = 127$ m.
- Charge in a magnetic field (Taylor 2.5): $\mathbf B = (0,0,B)$ points toward the viewer in the top view; $\omega = qB/m$ (signed, a negative charge turns counterclockwise), $\eta = v_x + iv_y$, $\dot\eta = -i\omega\eta$, radius $r = v/\omega = mv/(qB)$ (Taylor writes $r$, $v$ = transverse speed), period $2\pi/|\omega|$; positive charge turns clockwise seen from $+z$.
- Magnetic lesson data: electron in B = 0.010 T, v = 1.0e6 m/s: omega = 1.759e9 rad/s, r = 0.569 mm, T = 3.57 ns; with v_z = 2e5 m/s pitch 0.714 mm. Widget toy: q = m = B = v0 = 1, units removed.
- Challenge part 2 (toy data): sphere m = 1, b = 1, c = 0.1: v_ter = 6.09 m/s (between 9.8 linear-only and 9.90 quad-only), cv/b = 0.5 at 5 m/s, terms equal at b/c = 10 m/s, linear term dominates throughout the fall; electron helix B = 0.010 T, v = 1e6, v_z = 2e5: r 0.569 mm, pitch 0.714 mm, B doubled halves both.
- Challenge part 1 (toy data): ball m = 1 kg, b = 2 kg/s (tau 0.5 s, v_ter 4.9) thrown up at 10 m/s: peak 0.556 s, 2.275 m, fall 0.878 s, lands at 4.05 m/s; projectile m = 1, b = 0.2, 20 m/s at 45 deg (tau 5, v_ter 49): R_vac 40.82, formula 25.1, exact 29.11, x_inf 70.7, apex x 15.8 m; charge omega = 4/s, 1/tau = 3/s, v0 = 10: eta = v0 e^{-(1/tau + i omega)t}, path 3.33 m, end (1.2, -1.6) m, |.| = 2 m.

## Chapter 4
- Center of mass (Taylor 3.3): $\alpha$ labels particles, $M=\sum m_\alpha$, $\mathbf R=\frac1M\sum m_\alpha\mathbf r_\alpha$, $\mathbf P=M\dot{\mathbf R}$, $\mathbf F^{\text{ext}}=M\ddot{\mathbf R}$. Toy hammer: 1 kg handle at 0, 3 kg head at 1.2 m (CM 0.9 m), launch 7 m/s at 55 deg (range 4.70 m, flight 1.170 s), omega 4 rad/s (rod force 14.4 N each end).
- Rockets (Taylor 3.2): $m(t)$ rocket mass, $v$ velocity (forward positive), $v_{\text{ex}} > 0$ exhaust speed relative to the rocket, $dm < 0$; $m\,dv = -v_{\text{ex}}\,dm$, $m\dot v = -\dot m\,v_{\text{ex}}$, thrust $= -\dot m\,v_{\text{ex}}$, $v - v_0 = v_{\text{ex}}\ln(m_0/m)$; with gravity (up positive) $m\dot v = -\dot m\,v_{\text{ex}} - mg$, $v = v_{\text{ex}}\ln(m_0/m) - gt$. Fixed ground-frame exhaust gives $v = v_{\text{ex}}(m_0/m - 1)$.
- Toy rocket (widget): $m_0 = 100$ kg, $m_{\text{dry}} = 20$ kg, $-\dot m = 10$ kg/s, $v_{\text{ex}} = 2$ m/s ($v = 3.219$ m/s); gravity preset $g = 0.2$, $v_{\text{ex}} = 4$ ($v = 4.838$). Numbers: half mass gone $0.693\,v_{\text{ex}}$; 90% fuel $2.30\,v_{\text{ex}}$; Taylor's shuttle data thrust/weight $= 1.28$.
- Angular momentum (Taylor 3.4-3.5): single particle $\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{p}$ (Taylor $\boldsymbol\ell$), torque $\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$ (Taylor $\boldsymbol\Gamma$), $\dot{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{N}$, $dA/dt=L/2m$; plane $N_z=xF_y-yF_x$, $L_z=m(xv_y-yv_x)$, so a free line above $O$ moving $+x$ has $L_z=-mbv$ (clockwise, into the page). Toy: line $b=0.8$, $v=1$, $m=1$ gives $L=0.8$, $dA/dt=0.4$; push pulse $F=1$ along $+y$, $t=1$ to $2$, gives $\Delta L_z=-1$. Torque $\mathbf N$ shares its letter with the normal force of Level 2; context and the word "torque" tell them apart, and the torque is always $\mathbf N=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$ (never $\boldsymbol\Gamma$ in lessons, except "Taylor writes"). Several particles: total $\mathbf{L}=\sum\mathbf{r}_\alpha\times\mathbf{p}_\alpha$, $\dot{\mathbf{L}}=\mathbf{N}^{\text{ext}}$ (proof in the next lesson).
- Many-particle proof (Taylor 3.5): $\mathbf F_{\alpha\beta}$ = force on $\alpha$ by $\beta$, $\mathbf r_{\alpha\beta}=\mathbf r_\alpha-\mathbf r_\beta$, $\mathbf L_\alpha$ (Taylor $\boldsymbol\ell_\alpha$), $\mathbf N^{\text{ext}}$ (Taylor $\boldsymbol\Gamma^{\text{ext}}$); third law enters at the pairing, centrality at the zero cross product. Numbers: couple pair at $(0,0),(2,0)$ with $\pm3$ N sideways gives 6 N m; central 5 N pair at $(1,0),(0,2)$ internal torques $\pm2\sqrt5$, $N^{\text{ext}}=4$; skaters $2mr^2\omega$, 2 m to 1 m gives $\omega=4$.
- Challenge part 1: toy rocket 1000 kg (600 kg fuel), 20 kg/s, v_ex 2000 m/s, burnout 1538.6 m/s with g = 9.8; shell 3 kg at 40 m/s, 60 deg, burst at the top (70.7 m) into 1 and 2 kg, CM lands 141.4 m; puck 0.5 kg on a string, r 1.0 to 0.4 m, 2 to 5 m/s, L = 1, T = L^2/(m r^3).

## Chapter 5
- Work and kinetic energy (Taylor 4.1, Goldstein 1.1-1.2): $dW=\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$, $W(1\to2)=\int_1^2\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r$ (Goldstein writes $d\mathbf s$, $W_{12}$), $T=\tfrac12 mv^2$, $T_2-T_1=W(1\to2)$, net force = sum of works. Example force $\mathbf F=(y,2x)$ N from $O=(0,0)$ to $P=(1,1)$ m: road a (axis then up) 2 J, b (diagonal) 1.5 J, c (quarter circle about $Q=(1,0)$) $2-\pi/4=1.21$ J; 2 kg bead from rest arrives at $\sqrt2$, $\sqrt{1.5}$, 1.10 m/s. Widget room: A=(-1.2,-0.5), B=(1.2,0.5); steady push (1,2) gives 4.4 J, swirl k=1 about (0,1.5) straight 3.6 and bent 6.0, shear k=1 straight 0 and bent -1.2.
- Conservative forces and potential energy (Taylor 4.2-4.3, Goldstein 1.2): potential energy is $V$ (Goldstein; Taylor writes $U$, said once in the lesson), reference point $\mathbf r_0$, $V(\mathbf r)=-\int_{\mathbf r_0}^{\mathbf r}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r'$, $W(1\to2)=V_1-V_2=-\Delta V$, $E=T+V$, $\Delta E=W_{\text{nc}}$, $\mathbf F=-\nabla V$, $F_x=-\partial V/\partial x$. Conservative = (i) $\mathbf F=\mathbf F(\mathbf r)$ and (ii) road-independent work (equivalently zero work on closed roads; the magnetic force and normal force conserve $T$ but are not conservative). Numbers: 2 kg at 1.5 m gives $V=29.4$ J; spring $k=50$ N/m, $0.5$ kg from $0.2$ m gives 2 m/s at the centre; $V=x^2y+2y$ gives $\mathbf F=(-4,-6)$ N at $(2,1)$; swirl $(-y,x)$ round the unit circle $2\pi$ J; block 1 kg, 2 m slide, 1 m drop, friction 3 N, $v=\sqrt{7.6}=2.76$ m/s. Widget room as before; spring $k=1.5$ about $(0.2,0.1)$ gives 0.87 J A to B.
